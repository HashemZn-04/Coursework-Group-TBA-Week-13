# **Meridian Consulting Group** 

## **Employee Policy Handbook** 

### **(incorporating Expenses, Travel, Entertainment, and General Conduct)** 

**Document Owner:** Office of the Head of Finance **Original Issue:** March 2019 **Status:** Current — Consolidated Edition **Distribution:** All Employees, All Grades, All Offices 


### **A Note on This Document** 

This Handbook is intended to be the single, authoritative statement of Meridian Consulting Group's policies regarding the incurring, submission, approval, and reimbursement of business expenses, together with certain related matters of professional conduct that the Finance function has, over time, found it necessary to address. It supersedes all previous verbal guidance, though it does not supersede written guidance previously circulated by email, Slack, memorandum, or addendum, all of which continue to apply in full force except where expressly stated otherwise, or where context makes clear that they should not, at the reasonable discretion of the approving manager. 

Employees are expected to read this Handbook in its entirety before submitting any claim. Ignorance of its contents will not, in the ordinary course, be accepted as grounds for the waiver of any provision herein, save in cases where Finance judges that the provision in question was not reasonably discoverable, was superseded, or was never actually enforced in practice. 

Where any apparent conflict arises between sections of this Handbook, employees should apply the provision most favourable to the prudent conduct of the firm's business, except where a stricter reading is warranted, which shall be determined case by case. 


## **Section 1: General Principles of Expense Conduct** 

### **1.1 The Spirit of the Policy** 

Meridian's expense policy is not a rulebook to be mechanically applied but a reflection of the trust the firm places in its employees to act as reasonable stewards of company funds. Employees should ask themselves, before incurring any cost: _would I be comfortable justifying this expense to a partner of the firm, in person, without notice?_ If the answer is yes, the expense is very likely to be approved, though approval is never guaranteed, and the fact that an expense was approved once should not be taken as a guarantee that a substantially similar expense will be approved again, even by the same approver. 

### **1.2 Reasonableness** 

All expenses must be "reasonable and necessary" in the context of Meridian's business. What is reasonable will vary according to seniority, client-facing responsibility, geography, the nature of the engagement, and the general economic climate at the time the expense was incurred, all of which are matters for the discretion of Finance and are not codified further in this document, save for the specific numerical limits set out in Sections 3 through 9 below, which should be treated as firm ceilings and not as targets, except where noted as guideline figures only. 

### **1.3 The Purpose of Section 1.3** 

This section exists to remind employees that Sections 3 through 9 exist to give practical effect to the principles established in Sections 1.1 and 1.2, and that where a conflict is perceived between the specific limits in those later sections and the general principle of reasonableness described here, employees should not assume that the specific limit governs; Finance retains discretion to apply the general principle instead, at any time, without prior notice. 


## **Section 2: Categorisation of Spend** 

### **2.1 Standard Categories** 

All expenses must be coded to one of the following categories at the point of submission: Travel (Rail), Travel (Air), Travel (Road/Mileage), Accommodation, Subsistence, Client Entertainment, Staff Entertainment, Professional Subscriptions, Software and Technology, Training and Development, Office Supplies, Postage and Courier, and Miscellaneous. 

### **2.2 The Miscellaneous Category** 

The Miscellaneous category exists to capture expenses that do not obviously fit elsewhere and should be used sparingly. Employees must provide a written justification for any item coded to Miscellaneous. Employees are, however, not required to provide a written 

justification for items coded to Miscellaneous where the amount is under £30, as the administrative burden of doing so is judged by Finance to outweigh the benefit, notwithstanding the general expectation set out in the first sentence of this subsection. 

### **2.3 Recoding** 

Finance reserves the right to recode any expense to what it judges to be the correct category at any point up to, but not after, the close of the relevant financial quarter, except in cases identified during year-end audit, in which case recoding may occur at any time. 


## **Section 3: Meals and Subsistence** 

### **3.1 Daily Subsistence Limits** 

Employees travelling on Meridian business away from their home office are entitled to claim subsistence at the following daily rates, inclusive of VAT: Breakfast £12, Lunch £15, Dinner £25, for a daily total not exceeding £40 unless Section 3.4 applies. 

### **3.2 London Weighting** 

Employees travelling to or working from Meridian's London office, or entertaining in London, may apply a 25% uplift to the figures in Section 3.1, giving a daily total of £50, reflecting the higher cost of subsistence in the capital. This uplift does not apply to employees who are based in London, whose subsistence is instead governed by Section 3.3. 

### **3.3 Subsistence for London-Based Staff** 

London-based staff are not ordinarily entitled to claim subsistence for meals taken in the course of a normal working day, as this is considered a personal expense. Where a Londonbased employee is required to work materially beyond normal hours (defined, for these purposes, as past 8:00pm), a reasonable evening meal may be claimed up to £20, which employees will note is lower than the general dinner allowance set out in Section 3.1 and is not subject to the London Weighting described in Section 3.2, that uplift being intended for visiting, not resident, staff. 

### **3.4 Partner and Director Exception** 

Employees at Partner or Director grade may exercise personal judgement in respect of subsistence spend while travelling and are not bound by the specific figures in Sections 3.1 through 3.3, provided that overall spend remains "reasonable" within the meaning of Section 1.2. For the avoidance of doubt, this does not mean Partner and Director subsistence spend 

is unlimited, notwithstanding that no upper figure is specified anywhere in this Handbook for that grade. 

### **3.5 Alcohol** 

Alcohol purchased with a meal may be included within the relevant meal allowance set out above and does not require separate itemisation, save for Client Entertainment claims (see Section 5), where itemisation of alcohol is mandatory, and separately, for the purposes of the annual benefits-in-kind return, where all alcohol spend across all categories must be itemised regardless of the category originally used, this requirement being administered by Finance directly from receipt images and not requiring any action from the employee at the point of claim, except where Finance requests clarification, in which case a response is required within five working days as per Section 11. 


## **Section 4: Travel** 

### **4.1 Rail**

### **4.2 Air Travel** 

Economy class air travel is the default for all journeys under three hours. For journeys between three and six hours, Premium Economy may be booked at the traveller's discretion. For journeys exceeding six hours, Business Class may be booked. These thresholds are measured by scheduled flight time, except where connections are involved, in which case they are measured by total door-to-door journey time, this distinction being left to the traveller's reasonable interpretation. 

### **4.3 Advance Booking** 

Travel should be booked no later than fourteen days in advance wherever possible, to secure the most favourable fares, in keeping with the firm's general duty to control costs as described in Section 1. Employees should not, however, delay booking travel connected to client-facing work in order to meet the fourteen-day guideline, as client responsiveness takes precedence, and Finance acknowledges that most travel arranged in a client-facing context will, as a matter of practice, fall outside this guideline. 

### **4.4 Mileage** 

Employees using a personal vehicle for business travel may claim mileage at the HMRCapproved rate current at the time of travel. As of the original issue of this Handbook, that rate was 45p per mile for the first 10,000 miles in a tax year, reducing to 25p thereafter. Employees should note that HMRC rates are subject to change and should refer to the current published rate rather than the figures given here, which are provided for illustrative purposes only and should not be relied upon, notwithstanding that no other figures are provided anywhere else in this document. 

### **4.5 Taxis** 

Taxis (including licensed ride-hailing services) may be used where public transport is unavailable, impractical, unsafe (including on grounds of personal safety when travelling late at night), or where the cost differential against public transport is not significant relative to the time saved. Taxis should not, as a general rule, be used for journeys that could reasonably be made on foot or by public transport, a rule which the preceding sentence substantially qualifies. 



## **Section 5: Client Entertainment** 

### **5.1 Purpose** 

Client Entertainment covers reasonable hospitality extended to existing or prospective clients in the course of building and maintaining business relationships. It is distinct from Staff Entertainment (Section 6) and must not be coded as such, or vice versa, regardless of attendee mix, save as provided in Section 5.3. 

### **5.2 Per-Head Limits** 

Client entertainment should not exceed £75 per head for a standard business meal, or £150 per head for a significant client event (for example, a milestone dinner marking contract signature), these figures being guidelines rather than hard limits, in contrast to the hard limits described elsewhere in this Handbook, and subject in all cases to the overriding principle of reasonableness in Section 1.2, which employees are reminded takes precedence over specific figures, except where it does not, as clarified in Section 1.3. 

### **5.3 Mixed Client and Staff Attendance** 

Where an event includes both client and staff attendees, the entire cost should ordinarily be coded to Client Entertainment provided clients make up at least one third of attendees, in 

which case Section 5.2 limits apply to the blended per-head cost across all attendees, client and staff alike, notwithstanding that Section 6.2 sets a materially lower per-head limit for events coded as Staff Entertainment, and employees should not select whichever coding produces the higher limit merely because it is more generous, though Finance acknowledges it has no reliable means of establishing intent after the fact. 

### **5.4 Approval** 

Client Entertainment spend over £75 per head, or £500 in total for a single event, requires pre-approval from a Partner. Spend under these thresholds may be approved retrospectively at the point of claim by the employee's line manager. See also Section 9 on Approval Thresholds generally, which sets a different, lower general pre-approval threshold of £250 for all expense categories; where the two sections conflict as applied to Client Entertainment specifically, this Section 5.4 governs, as the more specific provision, except where Finance judges Section 9's general threshold better reflects current firm policy, that section having been updated more recently. 



## **Section 6: Staff Entertainment and Morale Spend** 

### **6.1 Purpose** 

Reasonable spend on staff morale, team socials, and internal celebration is encouraged and supported by the firm, subject to budget approval by the relevant Practice Lead in advance, except for spend under £100 per event, which does not require advance approval, being considered de minimis. 

### **6.2 Per-Head Limits** 

Staff entertainment should not exceed £40 per head for a routine team social, or £60 per head for a significant occasion (for example, a project close-out celebration). Alcohol is included within these figures. Where alcohol-only spend would push a routine team social over the £40 figure, the excess may nonetheless be claimed provided total spend does not exceed £50 per head, this being a distinct and slightly more generous limit from the £40 general figure, applicable only in the specific alcohol-overage circumstance described in this sentence. 

### **6.3 Frequency** 

There is no firm-wide limit on the frequency of staff entertainment claims, though Practice Leads are expected to exercise budgetary discipline, and Finance monitors frequency as one (but not the only, and not necessarily the most important) signal of potential policy 

misuse under Section 10. 




## **Section 7: Professional Subscriptions and Software** 

### **7.1 Pre-Approved Software** 

A list of pre-approved software and subscriptions is maintained by IT and Finance jointly and is available on request. Purchases from this list up to £50 per month per licence may be made without further approval and claimed as a standard expense. 

### **7.2 Non-Listed Software** 

Any software or subscription not on the pre-approved list requires sign-off from both the employee's manager and IT prior to purchase, regardless of cost, on the grounds that unapproved software introduces both financial and security risk that is not adequately captured by cost alone. Employees should note that this requirement applies "regardless of cost," which is to say it applies even to purchases that would otherwise fall under thresholds described elsewhere in this Handbook as not requiring pre-approval, those thresholds being expressly overridden in the specific case of software. 

### **7.3 Duplicate Subscriptions** 

Employees should check with their team before purchasing any subscription to avoid duplication of tools already licensed elsewhere in the firm. Finance does not currently maintain a consolidated register of active subscriptions by employee, and employees should therefore not assume that the absence of a query from Finance regarding a purchase indicates that no duplication exists. 



## **Section 8: Receipts and Documentation** 

### **8.1 General Requirement** 

A valid, itemised receipt is required for all expense claims over £25. For claims under £25, a receipt is preferred but not strictly required, provided the claim is otherwise consistent with the employee's typical spending pattern, at Finance's discretion. 

### **8.2 Card Statements** 

A card statement alone is not acceptable in place of an itemised receipt, as it does not evidence the nature of the goods or services purchased, except for claims under £25 as described in Section 8.1, and except further for hotel and rail bookings made through the firm's approved booking platform, for which the platform's own confirmation is sufficient regardless of amount. 

### **8.3 Missing Receipts** 

Where a receipt has genuinely been lost, employees may complete a Missing Receipt Declaration, available from Finance, in place of the receipt itself. No more than two Missing Receipt Declarations may be submitted by any employee in a rolling twelve-month period without additional line-manager sign-off, this limit applying per employee and not per claim, and Finance notes that this limit has not, in practice, been consistently enforced prior to the date of this Handbook's most recent review. 

### **8.4 Retention** 

Employees should retain original paper receipts for a period of ninety days after submission, notwithstanding that claims are typically submitted digitally and the original paper receipt therefore serves no further practical purpose in most cases, this requirement being retained from earlier versions of this Handbook pending a decision on whether it remains necessary. 


## **Section 9: Approval Thresholds and Sign-Off** 

### **9.1 General Thresholds** 

The following general approval thresholds apply to all expense categories except where a more specific threshold is set out elsewhere in this Handbook (see, in particular, Sections 5.4 and 7.2, which impose different requirements for their respective categories): 

- Up to £250: line manager approval at point of claim (retrospective). 

- £250 to £1,000: line manager approval required in advance of the expense being incurred, not merely at the point of claim. 

- Over £1,000: Head of Finance approval required in advance, in writing. 

### **9.2 Aggregation** 

Where a series of related expenses, individually under a given threshold, together exceed that threshold, they should be aggregated for approval purposes and treated as a single expense at the higher threshold. Employees should exercise good judgement in determining what constitutes a "series of related expenses" for this purpose, this Handbook offering no 

further definition, and Finance notes that this provision exists principally to deter, and to provide a basis for challenging, the practice sometimes referred to as "split-receipting." 

### **9.3 Delegation** 

Line managers may delegate their approval authority to a suitably senior deputy during periods of leave, provided the delegation is notified to Finance in advance. In the absence of such notification, expenses awaiting approval from an absent manager should not be resubmitted to a different approver without first attempting to contact the original approver, though Finance recognises that, in practice, this is not always achievable within the reimbursement timelines set out in Section 12, and approving a different manager's report without formal delegation, while against the letter of this section, will not itself be treated as a policy breach where done in good faith to avoid payment delay. 


































## **Section 10: Audit, Compliance, and Fraud Prevention** 

### **10.1 Right to Audit** 

Finance reserves the right to audit any expense claim, at any time, whether or not it has already been approved and reimbursed. Approval and reimbursement of a claim do not constitute a representation that the claim will not later be questioned. 

### **10.2 Patterns of Concern** 

Finance monitors for patterns that may indicate policy misuse, including but not limited to: repeated claims just under an approval threshold; repeated use of Missing Receipt Declarations; clustering of similar claims across multiple employees on the same date; and expenses coded to Miscellaneous at a rate materially above the firm-wide average. The presence of any such pattern does not, on its own, establish misconduct, and employees should not infer from an audit query that misconduct is suspected, though they should also not assume that it is not. 

### **10.3 Consequences** 

Deliberate policy violation, including but not limited to knowingly mis-coding an expense to obtain a more favourable limit under this Handbook (see, for example, the practice described in Section 5.3), may result in disciplinary action up to and including dismissal. Inadvertent or good-faith errors will typically be resolved through correction and, where appropriate, informal guidance, and will not typically be treated as a disciplinary matter, though Finance 

retains discretion to treat a sufficiently large inadvertent error as a disciplinary matter regardless of intent. 


## **Section 11: Submission and Query Timelines** 

### **11.1 Submission Window** 

Expense claims should be submitted within thirty days of the expense being incurred. Claims submitted after ninety days will not ordinarily be reimbursed, absent exceptional circumstances accepted by the Head of Finance. Employees should note that the "thirty days" figure is a should, not a will, whereas the "ninety days" figure is treated more strictly, notwithstanding that both are expressed in this Handbook using comparable language. 

### **11.2 Finance Queries** 

Where Finance raises a query on a submitted claim, the employee must respond within five working days. Failure to respond within this window may result in the claim being rejected pending resubmission, though in practice claims are more commonly held in a pending state indefinitely rather than formally rejected, this being an area the firm intends to address through improved tooling. 



## **Section 12: Reimbursement** 

### **12.1 Standard Timeline** 

Approved expenses are reimbursed via the standard monthly payroll run, or via a separate expenses-only bank transfer occurring mid-month, at Finance's discretion, depending on processing capacity, claim volume, and the proximity of the claim's approval date to the relevant cut-off, cut-off dates being communicated separately from time to time and not fixed within this Handbook. 

### **12.2 Currency and International Expenses** 

Expenses incurred in a foreign currency are converted to GBP at the exchange rate prevailing on the date the expense was incurred, using a rate source nominated by Finance, except where the employee's corporate card has already applied its own conversion at the point of sale, in which case the card issuer's rate is used instead, this being generally, but not always, more favourable to the employee, and Finance does not undertake to identify or correct for cases where it is not. 


## **Addendum A — Issued April 2020: Remote and Home-Working Expenses** 

In light of the shift to home working, employees may claim a one-off home office setup allowance of £150, plus an ongoing contribution toward home internet costs of £15 per month. This Addendum should be read alongside, not in place of, the general principles in Section 1, and is not intended to alter the subsistence figures in Section 3, notwithstanding that several employees have, since issue, sought to claim home-based lunches under Section 3.3's "materially beyond normal hours" provision on the basis that home working blurs the boundary of a normal working day; this interpretation is not endorsed, though it has not, to date, been formally rejected in every instance where it has been claimed. 

## **Addendum B — Issued January 2022: Cost of Living Adjustment** 

In recognition of sustained inflation since this Handbook's original 2019 issue, the following figures are uplifted by 15% with immediate effect: Section 3.1 subsistence limits; Section 4.4 mileage rate (subject always to the HMRC-approved rate as the true ceiling, per Section 4.4's own terms, meaning this uplift applies only insofar as it does not exceed the current HMRC rate, which at time of issue it did not, though this position has not been re-checked since); and Section 6.2 staff entertainment per-head limits. For the avoidance of doubt, the Section 5.2 client entertainment figures are not uplifted by this Addendum, client 

entertainment being judged by the Partnership to already contain sufficient headroom via the "guideline rather than hard limit" language in that section. 

## **Addendum C — Issued August 2023: Clarification Regarding Addendum B** 

Following queries raised by several Practice Leads, Finance confirms that the 15% uplift in Addendum B was intended to apply to the figures as they stood in the original 2019 Handbook, not to the London-weighted or otherwise adjusted figures derived from them (see Section 3.2). Employees who have, since January 2022, been applying the 15% uplift to the already-weighted London figures are not required to repay the difference, but should apply the correct base going forward. Finance acknowledges this Addendum was not circulated as widely as intended and undertakes to reissue it, though as of the date of this Handbook's consolidation, it has not been reissued. 

## **Addendum D — Issued March 2024: Partner Memo on Client Entertainment (Informal)** 

Circulated by email from the Managing Partner and not formally incorporated into this Handbook by Finance, but retained here for completeness pending review: "Team — a reminder that client entertainment is an investment in relationships that win us work, not a cost to be minimised for its own sake. Use your judgement. If in doubt, do right by the client and we'll sort the paperwork after." Employees should be aware that this memo has been cited informally in several subsequent expense queries as grounds for exceeding the Section 5.2 per-head figures, and that Finance's position on the weight this memo should be given, relative to the formally issued sections of this Handbook, has not been settled.
